# Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR) Control & Management Guidelines

## 1. Disease Profile & Symptoms
Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR), also known as "Goat Plague", is an acute, contagious viral disease of sheep and goats caused by a Morbillivirus.

### Observable Symptoms
- High fever (104°F - 106°F) lasting 3 to 5 days.
- Serous to mucopurulent nasal and ocular discharge leading to crusting around nostrils and eyes.
- Ulcerative stomatitis (sores inside mouth, eroded gums, foul breath).
- Severe necrotizing enteritis resulting in watery, fetid, blood-stained diarrhea.
- Severe dyspnea (labored breathing) and coughing due to secondary pneumonia.
- Dehydration and rapid emaciation leading to mortality rate up to 80-90% in unvaccinated flocks.

---

## 2. Containment & Biosecurity Protocols
1. **Flock Isolation**: Immediately segregate sick sheep and goats into a dry, well-ventilated isolation bay.
2. **Prevent Mixed Grazing**: Halt communal pasture grazing immediately to stop horizontal aerosol transmission.
3. **Premises Sanitation**: Spray premises with 2% Phenol or 1% Sodium Hypochlorite. Burn or deeply bury all contaminated litter and feces with quicklime.

---

## 3. Supportive Treatment Protocols
- **Oral Care**: Flush mouth sores with warm 1% Alum or Potassium Permanganate solution twice daily.
- **Hydration & Nutrition**: Administer Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) with dextrose. Feed soft palatable green leaves or gruel.
- **Antibiotic Umbrella**: Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., Long-acting Oxytetracycline @ 20 mg/kg IM) under veterinary supervision to prevent secondary bacterial pneumonia.
- **Antipyretics**: Meloxicam or Paracetamol for fever management.

---

## 4. Vaccination & Eradication
- Administer PPR Live Attenuated Vaccine (Sungri 96 strain) to all healthy goats and sheep above 3 months of age.
- Provides long-lasting immunity for up to 3 years.
